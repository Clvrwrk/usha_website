## Section 1 (dark surface)

EYEBROW: Industry
# Great technology needs a market.
You can build the best electrolyzer, fuel cell, storage system, production process, infrastructure, or hydrogen project in the world. But technology alone does not create an economy.
Markets require customers. Customers require reliable supply, infrastructure, pricing, and demand. Investment requires certainty. Communities need benefits. Local and national policy sets the rules for all of it.
USHA works where those pieces meet.

## Section 5 (dark surface)

EYEBROW: Fifty states
## Fifty states change the equation.
Hydrogen markets don’t develop uniformly. Each state represents different resources, industries, drivers, policies, and market conditions.
A national hydrogen economy requires understanding those differences and connecting them. USHA is the only hydrogen business association working state by state, and the only one connecting what each state builds into one market.
